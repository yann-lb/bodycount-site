Try BodyCount before everyone else

Before a new app can launch on Google Play, at least twelve people have to test it for two weeks. That’s where you come in.

Sign me up

Two minutes. Test, get notified, or both. The form is in French for now — three simple questions.

How it works

  1. You sign up with your Google account address — the one your Play Store uses, often a Gmail address. That’s what Google uses to give you access.
  2. You get an invitation link by email. It opens the BodyCount page on Google Play, visible only to you.
  3. You install the app and keep it for fourteen days. That’s the only real requirement: Google counts the testers who still have it installed after two weeks.
  4. You use it however you like, and tell me what’s broken if you feel like it. No reports required.

What you need

  • An Android phone. No iPhone version yet.
  • A Google account, the one on your Play Store.
  • To be 18 or older. BodyCount is about sex life and sexual health.
  • To be fine with an app in French: there’s no English version of the app yet.
Testing doesn’t expose you to anything: the app creates no account, has no server, and doesn’t even have permission to access the internet. What you write in it stays on your phone, encrypted — I can’t see any of it, tester or not.

Just want to hear about it?

Same form: only tick “get notified about what’s new.” One email at launch on Google Play and for major updates, nothing more.

Your email address

It’s used to invite you to the test and, if you asked, to tell you about what’s new. Nothing else: it’s not sold, not shared, not used for advertising. The form is hosted by Google (Google Forms). To unsubscribe or have your address deleted, just send a line to ylb.applicationfr@gmail.com. The details are in the privacy policy.
